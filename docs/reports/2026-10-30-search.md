# Item 11: the search, detector and persistence (no model calls)

Every number below is copied from a file under `out/search/` (git-ignored, as out/baseline_v1/ was), named
beside it. The commands at the end rebuild each file. No model was called. No grade was raised: every survivor
keeps the detector's grade, "needs second look". Nothing here is a candidate yet.

## What ran

The frozen-v1 detector over all 34 `mpl_summer` strips, the 15 strips from 2007 first and then the rest in plan
order, one file per strip (`out/search/candidates/candidates_<id>.json`). Each file records detector v0.4,
`frozen: true`, table calibration with landers/calibration_v0.json, landers/shape_v3.json and saturated pixels
as data. That is the held-out run's `detector` record field for field (compared after a JSON round trip).

Code state. HEAD carries no tag, so the run used a new `--same-code-as frozen-v1`. It refuses unless `git diff
frozen-v1 HEAD` is empty over landers/ (minus landers/search/), scripts/detect.py, controls/thresholds.json,
pyproject.toml and uv.lock, the same set the held-out run compared. Each file records its commit and
`same_code_as`. The strips ran at ca27f97 (the first 15) and 4f30570 (the other 19). The two commits differ
only in landers/search/ and scripts/search.py, which drive the detector and are not part of it.

One fix before the run. The 11a driver built `DetectorConfig(frozen=True, calibration=...)` without the shape
rules, so it would have run a detector that differs from frozen-v1. It now builds the config that
`scripts/detect.py --frozen` builds with its defaults, and a test checks this.

Totals (`out/search/survivors.json`, `per_strip`): 14,589 tiles, 2975.34 km2 of valid ground, 2,544,629
detector candidates, 36,105 s of detector time with three workers.

What the table says. The calibrated score is meant to mean the same false-alarm rate everywhere. On this
ground it does not. Candidates per km2 run from 0.39 to 3562.81, and the score of the 2-per-km2 cut runs from
-2.106 to 2.22. This is the per-terrain calibration problem of item 8(a), which was not adopted. It is why the
survivors are cut per strip by count and not by one global score. A global cut would hand most of the list to
PSP_005391_1030 and PSP_005958_1025.

By branch (`survivors.json`, `by_branch`): sheet 2,758, bright 1,356, dark 926, dark_compact 710, shadow 20.
Sheets are almost half the list. The sheet branch looks for an 8 to 25 m bright flat patch, and on polar
summer ground that may be what residual frost or bright polygon floors look like. That is a question for a
person looking at crops, not a finding.

## Persistence across dates

All 81 overlapping pairs from out/search/overlaps.json ran in both directions (`out/search/persist/`, one file
per pair). Each date was registered to the other with landers.persist.offset_field. Each survivor was mapped
across and looked up in the other date's whole candidate list with the control-site rule: same family within
8 px, which is 2 m at 0.25 m per pixel. A survivor whose mapped spot falls off the other strip's valid ground,
or which has no registered node near it, is "not looked at", never "not seen".

Chance. 2,658 survivor lookups on another date were made and 2,109 were seen. If the other dates' candidates
were spread evenly, 30.9 would have been seen by chance (`survivors.json`, `chance`). So the matches are not
chance, but that is not evidence for hardware. Rocks, frost patches and polygon features also stay put
between dates. Persistence is a test a lander must pass, and on this ground nearly everything else passes it
too. By branch, survivors looked at on another date and never seen there: dark 13 of 256, dark_compact 25 of
244, sheet 198 of 967, bright 200 of 730 (`by_branch`).

Registration is the weak point. 38 of the 81 pairs registered (at least 4 good nodes in one direction). They
hold 651.7 of the 1118.76 km2 of pairwise overlap (`survivors.json`, `pairs`). 79 of the 162 directions found
no good node at all (`registration`). Failures cluster on three strips:

- PSP_004336_1035 and PSP_004402_1030, the two earliest (2007-06-30 and 2007-07-05), fail against every
  partner, including the largest overlap in the set (ESP_013289_1035 with PSP_004336_1035, 75.32 km2). They
  are also the two strips with almost no detector candidates (300 and 39). Something about these two images
  differs from the rest. A whole-strip contrast measure does not show what (`out/search/strip_contrast.json`).
  PSP_004402_1030 is dark (median DN 129 on its overview), but ESP_013078_1030 is as dark (130) and registers
  with most of its partners. PSP_004336_1035 is not unusual on this measure. Atmospheric haze is a hypothesis
  to check by eye, not a finding.
- ESP_057965_1035, the 0.5 m strip, fails against every partner. That is expected from the code:
  `register_at` matches windows pixel for pixel and does not resample one scale to the other.

So the coverage figure below gives dates imaged and searched, which is more than dates compared. A survivor in
an unregistered pair counts as "not looked at" there.

## What I decided and why

- Survivors cut at the adopted 2 per km2, per strip. That makes a list a person can check, and the number is
  already the project's own. A global score cut would be unfair across strips while the calibration is
  uneven (above).
- Persistence never sets a grade in the search. `landers.persist.annotate` sets "candidate" when a match is
  seen, but a candidate also needs the physical tests (SAM masks, the decision table) that have not run on
  these survivors. The search calls annotate on copies and keeps only the evidence.
- Unregistered and off-ground lookups are "not looked at", so a failed registration can only cost coverage,
  never make a false match or a false absence.
- The detector ran from an untagged commit with a byte-for-byte check against the tag. The alternative,
  checking the tag out in a shared worktree, risked a later session finding a detached HEAD.

## Not done, and why

- Model calls on the survivors (SAM, Wonder votes, Spark review). The plan's decision of 10-29 says to stop
  before them for a person: they cost money and need an ask in the session. 5,770 survivors is far more than
  the 9c caps allow, so a person also has to choose how many and which.

## What I would do next

1. Registration. Resample the 0.5 m strip to 0.25 m before matching, and look at PSP_004336_1035 and
   PSP_004402_1030 by eye to see why they neither register nor yield candidates. This would add up to 467 km2
   of pairwise overlap to the persistence test.
2. Ask a person how the model stage should take the survivors. One option is the 5 survivors seen on 4 of 4
   dates and the 329 on 3 of 3 first, as the cheapest set with the most evidence. Another is the 9c budget
   applied per strip.
3. The calibration spread is the single biggest thing the list inherits. A per-strip or per-terrain fit
   (item 8(a), not adopted) is a frozen-v2 question for a person, and both lists would be shown.
